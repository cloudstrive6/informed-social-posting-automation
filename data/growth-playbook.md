# InforMed growth playbook: week of 2026-10-28

**Status: off track.** 45 of 4,000 watch hours (1.1%); 373 of 1,000 subs. Long-form median this week: 15.5 views (target 30+). Practical tirzepatide long-forms earned 91% of this week's new watch minutes. Every long-form slot goes to what earns minutes.

## Long-form (1 a day, 14:00 ET, 8–12 min)
1. **Lane C, fresh result (Exp C).** All of these must hold:
   - The result first became public within 48 h (check the first-report date).
   - It's a named next-gen drug, with a headline efficacy number from a randomized trial.
   - Best case: a tirzepatide or semaglutide arm, like EloraTZP.
   - Filings, Phase 1, food-effect or status-only news go to a Short instead.
   - Drug name first in the title. Label it `lane: C`.
2. **Lane A, practical tirzepatide (Exp A). The default on every other day.** This week, in this order:
   - "Tirzepatide and Your Bones and Muscle: What the Evidence Says". This is an allowed exception to the 14-day Short-repeat rule.
   - "Protein on Tirzepatide: How Much and Why"
   - "What Happens When You Stop Tirzepatide?" Use SURMOUNT-4 and say "don't stop or change your medicine on your own".
   - "Tirzepatide Hair Loss: How Common Is It and What Helps?"
   - "Tirzepatide Weight Loss Plateau: What the Trials Show"
   - Label each one `lane: A`.
- **Paused:** Lane P "What Is [drug]?" explainers (petrelintide had 4 views at day 2). This will be reviewed at day 7 for petrelintide and elecoglipron.
- **Banned in long-form:**
  - Cost, coverage, Medicare, pharmacy and gray-market topics.
  - Single-trial deep dives on approved drugs.
  - Timeline-only topics.
  - "Did X beat Y?" without a randomized head-to-head trial.
  - Broad head-term titles that big channels own (e.g. "Tirzepatide vs Semaglutide": 9 views).

## Long-form packaging and retention
- **Title:** the long-tail question a tirzepatide user would type, drug name first, about 60 characters or fewer, at most 1 number. It must be fully paid off in the video.
- **Thumbnail:** 4 words or fewer, never repeating the title. One labelled character, one honest emotion.
- **0–15 s:** open on a concrete "you" scenario and give the first answer. No trial-number openings and no setup.
- **Body:** re-hook every 45–75 s. Name chapters after searched questions. One subscribe ask, after the payoff.
- **Ending (new):** don't speak any video title. Say "Watch next: the video on screen now, linked at the top of the description." The publisher adds the verified "▶ Watch next" link, and the owner adds the end screen.

## Shorts (3 a day, at most 170 words, 60–85 s)
- **08:00, body worry:** a calm "honest uncertainty" Short (gallbladder, pancreas, hair, bone density, muscle). Tirzepatide in the title where the evidence covers it. It wins on both YouTube and Instagram.
- **13:00, lead.** Pick one:
  - a two-drug head-to-head with tirzepatide named first and a new trial each time;
  - a retatrutide Short with new Phase 3 data or a "what's confirmed?" fact, with a loop ending;
  - a gray-market "what's confirmed?" Short with a new dated FDA/MHRA/manufacturer fact (at most 1 a week).
- **20:00, funnel Short (Exp D, unchanged):** a fresh single question answered by a live long-form. Rotate: that day's Lane C video, then EloraTZP, then Strength Training on Tirzepatide.
  - Last spoken line: "Full breakdown on our channel, link in the description."
  - The publisher writes all links. Agents never write YouTube URLs or spoken titles.
- **Banned:**
  - "Is X approved yet?" titles.
  - "3 Ways…" lists.
  - Brand-first titles, and semaglutide-only titles (unless the evidence is semaglutide-only).
  - Cost or coverage Shorts.
  - Observational surgery angles.
  - Titles that name no drug.
  - Scare framing.
- **Variety:** any one trial in at most 1 Short a week, no back-to-back same-template titles, a new fact in every piece.

## Quality gates (non-negotiable)
- **Masking:** Shorts mix at 0.02 or lower the first time, at the long-form music level. Measure last. Above 0.03 is an automatic drop: 5 Shorts were lost this week.
- **Captions:** built from on-screen text. Numbers match their stat pills exactly; never split a number or trial name.
- **Safety lines:** every piece carries "Not personal advice" and "talk to your doctor or prescriber about whether it's right for you". No dosing, switching or stopping advice, and no compounded or online sources. The fact-checker and the Editor-in-Chief always win.

## Carousels (until the owner decides)
- Reuse verified Short text only, with zero new research.
