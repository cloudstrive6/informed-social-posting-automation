### Motion Designer
- **Shorts: keep the bottom ~35% of the frame free of labels. That space belongs to the caption pill.** Put "Stomach", drug-name and stat tags above or beside the hero art, never under the mascots. When the caption changes, fully clear the previous shot's labels. Overlap findings were 17 (S2 09-28), 19 (S2 09-30), 5 (10-01) and 23 across the three 10-05 Shorts. Ghost labels left behind new captions appeared in S2 09-30, S1 10-01 and S2 10-05 ("Muscle (est.)").
- **Every number in a caption must be numerals, and must match the stat pill exactly. Check each one before export.** Write "119", "1 in 802", "35.4%", "21.3%" and "47,039", never "AND NINETEEN", "ONE IN EIGHT" or "THIRTY-FIVE POINT FOUR". Never split a number across two caption screens. Keep captions to 2 lines or fewer (S1 10-05 had two captions on 3 lines). Don't leave fragments ending in a comma. About 25 notes so far; S3 10-05 showed a factually wrong figure on screen.
- **Text must be at least ~40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label.** This covers chart pill labels ("STEP 2", "SYNCHRONIZE-2"), "X in 4" pictogram labels, delta tags, date chips and arrow labels. There have been 109 tiny-text findings in long-form, and the count is rising: 20 (L1 09-30), 15 (L1 10-01), 21 (L1 10-05).
- **Keep every box inside the safe frame: bottom edge ≤92% of frame height (y ≤ 994 on 1080, y ≤ 1766 on 1920), with a 5% margin at the sides and top.** This includes pills under characters and side labels. In L1 10-05 the "Placebo: 2 in…" label was cut off at the right edge. There have been 99 off-screen findings in long-form, with 4 on 10-05.
- **Never open on, or hold, a lone icon on a plain gradient, and keep one backdrop for the whole shot.** Every sampled frame needs a headline plus hero art or a scene covering at least ~25% of the frame. Recent cases: the lone "Investors" face and a newspaper pair floating on a gradient (L1 10-05), and a bare body outline on purple with no headline (S3 10-05). There have been 132 sparse findings in long-form.
- **Keep text off faces, the hero art and other elements.** Stat pills and qualifier lines go above or beside the hero. Charts get their own box and must never run through the number or its qualifier line. Recent cases: the "19 of 1,348" pill on the hero (S3 10-05) and emoji faces over "−35.4%" (S2 10-05). About 35 notes.

### Scriptwriters
- **Write narration in normal spelling, and write every stat as numerals ("119 hospitals", "1 in 802", "35.4%", "Phase 2b"). Never use phonetic respellings or numbers spelled out as words.** Subtitles are built from the script. Respellings caused 3 holds, the latest S1 10-01, and spelled-out numbers produced wrong on-screen numbers in S3 10-05. Reword any bare number that could be read as a year.
- **Every number needs its comparator, arm size and estimand, and every relative figure needs its absolute figure.**
  - Give the HR together with the absolute rates.
  - Put placebo next to every rate, including dropouts.
  - Give the size of each arm when one arm is small: placebo n=36 vs 124 (S2 10-05); 1,493 vs about 20,273 (S2 09-30).
  - Give the trial's estimated difference with its CI.
  - Say which way of counting you use ("everyone randomised" vs "if they stayed on treatment"), and don't compare across the two.
  - About 22 findings, including C1 and S2 10-05.
- **Report null and within-group results exactly.** A change seen within one group is not a difference vs placebo (C1 10-05). "No significant difference" is not "no drop" (C1 10-01). Not proven is not "a myth". Say whether a result is a primary, secondary or post hoc endpoint (S2 10-05, L1 10-01, S2 09-28). 6 findings.
- **State the limits the result depends on.** That means who was studied and for how long, the confounders the study did not adjust for (S3 10-05), and the limitations the authors or independent experts raise. Dropping an expert limitation caused the L1 09-27 hold. Comparisons across separate trials must be framed as such, never as a measured head-to-head (L1 and S1 10-05, L3 09-27). Our own reading must never be attributed to a source (S1 10-05 investors). Put the strongest evidence first. 12 findings.
- **Every scene carries a source ID. Never invent figures, and on-screen text can never be stronger or narrower than the narration.** Recent cases: S2 10-05 s6 had no source, and S1 10-05 dropped "overweight or obesity" on screen. Earlier cases: an invented time figure, a running count presented as final, and "harmless" where the source says "usually benign". 10 findings.

### Carousel Designer
- **Every stat, range, chart, pictogram or bubble needs a qualifier line saying what it counts.** That means raw vs placebo-adjusted, the comparator, the timeframe, the subgroup, modelled values with their range, or separate cohorts. Pictograms must not round up. A within-group change must not sit next to "vs placebo" results without saying so (C1 10-05). This also applies to Short stat pills and thumbnail numbers. 13 findings.
- **Every claim slide gets a source tag, and that source must actually say it.** Put the funder on any industry-funded trial slide (REMODEL was Novo Nordisk-funded). Never use a "24/7" layout for a helpline that isn't 24/7. The title must promise only what the slides deliver, including the less favourable estimand. 8 findings.

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube titles: 100 characters or fewer, and they must name the outcome, the dose or arm, the comparator and the full population.**
  - Name the outcome and arm: "13.1% weight loss on the top dose", not a bare "13.1%".
  - Say which number is placebo.
  - Keep "overweight or obesity" + T2D, never just "Obesity" (L1 10-05, L1 10-01).
  - No vague subjects (S3 10-05 didn't mention anaesthesia).
  - Use "linked to" for observational data, and the verified number rather than "up to".
  - Generic name first.
  - 1 hold (S2 09-30) and about 20 findings, 3 of them on 10-05.
- **YouTube description: 1,500 characters or fewer, counted before you submit. It was cut off in 9 of 10 long-form videos, again in L1 10-05 (mid-URL).**
  - Order: a quick answer of 3 lines or less → the disclaimer and prescriber line → up to 4 sources (short title + full URL) → chapters.
  - If space runs short, cut chapters before sources.
  - No "Key numbers" or "What you'll learn" lists.
  - The last line must be complete.
- **Fill every field and close every caption correctly.** That means the YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Threads captions (long-form too; 3 were missing on 09-28), the pinned comment and the thumbnail text. Each ends with the disclaimer plus exactly: "It's a prescription medicine. Talk to your doctor or prescriber about whether it's right for you." Include red-flag lines and helplines wherever there's room. Keep Threads bodies to about 350 characters so the closing lines fit under 500 (C1 and S1 10-05, C1 10-01, S3 09-29). About 15 findings.
- **Before writing, copy the key facts from the verified summary and use them identically everywhere:** sample, subgroup vs whole cohort, who had which condition, "and" vs "or", every arm, the outcome and the effect size. A scope mismatch means REVISE or a hold (S1 09-28 and S2 10-01 captions repeated the script's error).
- **Carry every qualifier, attribution, caveat and null result into every caption, and never make a claim stronger.**
  - Keep "on average", the full population, "Lilly reported", "Novo Nordisk-funded" (C1 10-05, twice), "not only muscle" (S2 10-05), "observational" and the placebo comparator (S1 10-05 TikTok).
  - Every caption that carries the benefit also carries the side-effect line.
  - The first line before the fold carries the hedge.
  - Engagement questions must not imply a head-to-head ("side-by-side") or the wrong comparator.
  - No "proven", "expert", "real result" or "slightly".
  - About 60 findings, 16 on 10-05.
- **Hashtags and tone: no #WeightLoss or #WeightLossJourney, no brand-name hashtags, and no hype emojis (🔥 👀).** Weight-loss and brand hashtags: C1 09-28, S1 10-05, S3 10-05. Hype emojis: S3 09-28, S3 10-01.
