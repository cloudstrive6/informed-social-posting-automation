### Motion Designer
- **Keep negations and status phrases on one caption screen.** "NOT CONFIRMED", "NOT RULED OUT" and "NO EVIDENCE PNEUMONIC PLAGUE PLAYED A PART" each stay together. Never end a caption on "NOT" or "NO". Before rendering, check every caption that contains "confirmed", "ruled out", "evidence" or "found". (6 cases from S2 10-07 to S2 10-09, 1 of them a hold. S1 10-10 still showed "AND IT'S NOT" on its own.)
- **Write captions as complete phrases of 2 lines or fewer.** Never end a caption on a comma or on a dangling word like "THE".
  - "CONTACT A DOCTOR OR LOCAL HEALTH AUTHORITY URGENTLY" is always one caption. It was split as "OR LOCAL HEALTH" in S2 10-09, S1 10-10 and S2 10-10.
  - Write dates and numbers as numerals that match the pill, such as "OCT 8, 2026" and "80%+".
  - Never split a label like "BSL-2".
  - (About 45 findings, including about 10 trailing commas on 10-10.)
- **Shorts: clear each tag before the next caption appears, and place tags only in the top 60% of the frame (y ≤ 1150 on 1920).** The caption pill owns the bottom 35%. If a tag can't be cleared in time, leave it out. The repeat offenders are "Lungs", "Antibiotic(s)", "Pneumonia", "Y. pestis" and "Stomach". (18 overlaps across the three 10-10 Shorts; leftover tags in 10+ Shorts.)
- **Make all text at least ~40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label.** This applies to Shorts and long-form. (90 tiny-text findings across L1, L2 and L4 10-10. L4's "Lymph node" and "Blood" labels were 22–24px, and S2 10-10's "Antibiotic" tag was 23px. About 270 tiny-text findings in long-form overall.)
- **Keep everything inside the safe frame.**
  - The bottom edge stays at y ≤ 994 on 1080 and y ≤ 1766 on 1920, with 5% margins at the sides and top.
  - Long words like "ROSPOTREBNADZOR" must fit their pill: shrink or wrap them.
  - Never crop a head, mascot or icon at the frame edge (S2 10-09, S3 10-10, L1 10-09).
  - (About 185 off-screen findings, including 19 in L4 10-10.)
- **Never open on, hold or end on a lone icon or a chips-only frame on a plain gradient.**
  - Every frame needs a headline plus hero art or a scene covering at least ~25% of the frame.
  - Use full scenes for lab, clinic and outro beats.
  - Examples: L1 10-10 had a lone globe, petri dish and lab-worker icon. L4 10-10 had frames at 5–6% coverage. S1 and S2 10-10 had sparse outros.
  - (About 205 sparse findings.)
- **Every scripted headline, status card and stat card must render in its own shot, and nothing from another shot may appear in it.**
  - Before rendering, compare each long-form shot with its scripted title. Compliance cards come first: "NOT CONFIRMED / WHO, [date]", dated update cards and the "5,000 tests (governor)" card. L2 10-10 dropped all of them.
  - Never let headlines, pills or scenes leak into another beat:
    - L4 10-10 showed "Rare / Bubonic plague, person to person (WHO)" and "Not every form spreads" about 250 s early. Its scripted beats "Hungry fleas move on" and "Pets can carry fleas home" never appeared.
    - L2 10-10 showed "Who Passes It On" about 230 s early.
    - L1 10-10 put the "Monitoring completed" clinic inside the Madagascar shot.
  - Put source sub-lines and stat titles above or beside the hero art, not on heads (S2 10-10).
  - (About 75 findings across 6 long-forms, 3 of them on 10-10.)

### Scriptwriters
- **Shorts: 170 words or fewer, including the dated status line.** Cut a beat rather than run long. (Over the limit in S1 and S2 10-09 and in S1, S2 and S3 10-10. S2 10-10 had about 235 words.)
- **Write narration in normal American spelling, with every stat and date in numerals** ("Yersinia pestis", "1 in 802", "Oct 8, 2026", "septicemic", "recognized"). Never use phonetic respellings. Captions and chapter cards are built from the script. This is how "OCTOBER EIGHTH" got on screen and how L4 10-10 ended up mixing "septicaemic" with "Septicemic". (4 holds.)
- **Use the lab-test wording from CDC's 2020 case definition.**
  - PCR, antigen, F1 or rapid tests = "presumptive" or "probable".
  - "Confirmed" = Y. pestis grown in culture, or a fourfold rise in antibodies.
  - Never cite CDC's "Clinical Testing and Diagnosis" page for PCR, antigen tests or culture time, because it doesn't cover them.
  - (2 holds: L1 10-07 and L1 10-10.)
- **Tie every claim to the exact form, product, place, group and scope its source covers, and use the source's own verbs.**
  - A finding about one vaccine, drug or dose arm never moves to another (5 scope holds).
  - Use the sources' wording: "requires direct and close contact", "can be considered", "Irkutsk oblast", "WHO European Region", "handling tissue or body fluids of an infected animal" (not "without gloves") and CDC's "can sometimes cough droplets" (keep "sometimes" and the animal half; L4 10-10).
  - Treatment: when crediting CDC, use CDC's exact sentence: "Plague can be cured with antibiotics, but treatment must be given quickly." (L3 10-10). Otherwise say "especially when started early". Never say "if early" (S3 10-09, S3 10-10).
  - WHO's 30–60% is "a case-fatality ratio for the bubonic type", not "kills 30–60% of people" (L4 10-10). For contacts, WHO says "seven-day chemoprophylaxis".
  - Never widen a claim about pneumonic plague to all plague. The governor's "no dangerous pathogens" covers the lab tests, not the contact monitoring.
- **Every number gets its comparator, timeframe and denominator. On-screen text is never stronger, narrower, shorter or more reassuring than the narration.**
  - Not confirmed ≠ ruled out ≠ "myth". Credit "not ruled out" to a body that says it, never to our own voice (L2 10-10).
  - On-screen cards keep the source's caveats. L3 10-10's governor card dropped AP's "did not explicitly say whether pneumonic plague had been ruled out".
  - Counts on screen match the narration (S3 10-09).
  - Keep "or local health authority" and the full symptom list (S3 10-10).
  - (About 75 findings.)

### Carousel Designer
- **Every stat or table cell needs a qualifier saying what it counts.** It keeps the source's hedge ("Can, with close contact", not "Yes") and cites the source that actually says it. Never present our own label as the agency's: "Early signs" (C1 10-10) and "in recent decades" (C1 10-09) are not CDC's words. (About 22 findings.)
- **If the title promises a source's list, the slides reproduce that whole list.**
  - C1 10-10 promised CDC's prevention steps, then dropped "reduce rodent habitat" and swapped in an item from another page.
  - C1 09-28 promised a checklist it never delivered.
  - Background facts need a source, and helplines show their real hours.
  - (About 15 findings.)

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube description: plain text, 1,500 characters or fewer measured after assembly, with a complete last line.**
  - Never include JSON, a literal "\n" or a cut-off URL.
  - If it's too long, cut chapters first, then the last source.
  - The order is:
    1. A quick answer of **3 lines or fewer** with the dated status hedge.
    2. The disclaimer plus "If you're worried, talk to a health professional."
    3. Up to 4 sources.
    4. Chapters.
  - Truncation is fixed in L1, L2 and L4 10-10. The quick answer is still too long (S1 10-10, L4 10-10, L1 10-01), and S1 10-10 had no sources.
  - (13 of 18 long-form descriptions were broken; 2 holds.)
- **Titles and thumbnails: 100 characters or fewer, starting with the words "Plague" or "Pneumonic plague", with at most one abbreviation.**
  - "Pneumonic vs…" (L4 10-10) splits the search phrase. Use "Pneumonic plague vs bubonic plague…" or "Plague: pneumonic vs bubonic…".
  - Status titles keep "suspected" or "reported" plus "as of [date]" ("Plague in Russia?" in L1 10-10 dropped it).
  - Thumbnails say "NOT CONFIRMED", never "UNCONFIRMED", and "not documented in the US since 1924", never "last".
  - Two abbreviations appeared in S2 10-10 and L1 10-09.
  - (1 hold, about 37 findings.)
- **Fill every field and end each one with the disclaimer plus the exact line "If you're worried, talk to a health professional."**
  - Threads breaks this most often. It lacked the exact line in C1, S1 and S2 10-10 and ran to 430–470 characters in C1 and S3 10-10. Keep Threads bodies to about 350 characters.
  - Every caption keeps "contact a doctor or local health authority urgently" and CDC's 4 pneumonic signs: fever, cough, shortness of breath and chest pain.
  - (About 35 findings.)
- **Copy facts, numbers and attributions word for word from the verified script.**
  - One kit stays one kit (S2 10-10, 4 captions).
  - "Handling an infected animal" never becomes "without gloves" (S3 10-10).
  - Keep "follow label", "played a part in", "Russian authorities" and "Reuters reports".
  - Script errors carried into captions have caused 5 holds.
- **Put the hedge and the newest dated status in the first line of every caption, before the fold.**
  - S1 and S2 10-10 had the Instagram hedge only in paragraph 4, or not at all.
  - S2 10-10 left out the Oct 9 governor update on YouTube and TikTok.
  - Never strengthen a claim: no "always", "at all", "proven", "exposed" (for "possible exposure") or "only recommends".
  - (About 95 findings.)
- **Engagement lines ask people to save or share, never to describe beliefs, sightings or what they've heard.**
  - No emojis in plague captions on Meta.
  - No #CDC, #WHO or #outbreak.
  - No "near you" or "any wild rodents" framing.
  - (Open-ended prompts flagged 5 times: S2 10-07, S3 10-09, S1 10-10, S3 10-10.)

### Everyone

  - Re-check every named body at publish time and put the newest dated development in the opening beat.
  - Include every dated development, even unresolved ones, and never end on the most reassuring line.

    - Oct 9: the governor update.
  - Never merge two days (L1 10-09, L1 10-10 pinned comment). Every status line carries a date (S1 10-10).
  - Quote each statement at its exact scope: "no evidence pneumonic plague played a part" ≠ "no evidence of plague".
