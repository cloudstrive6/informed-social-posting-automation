_Only 5 records are about plague (all 10-07), which is too few for plague-specific patterns. The rules below are the long-running ones, rewritten for plague. Plague-only points are marked "10-07" and should be treated as early signals. GLP-1-only details (trial populations, the prescriber line) were removed because the channel no longer covers that topic._

### Motion Designer
- **Shorts: keep the bottom ~35% of the frame free of labels, because that's where the caption pill goes. Fully clear the last shot's labels before the next caption appears.** Put tags ("Coughing", "Antibiotics", "Contacts tested", "WHO risk") above or beside the hero art. (Overlaps: 23 on 10-05, 22 on 10-06, 12 on 10-07, 9 of them in S1. Ghost labels in 5 Shorts.)
- **Caption numbers must be numerals that match the stat pill exactly ("80%+", not "OVER EIGHTY PERCENT"). Never split a number across screens. Keep captions to 2 lines or fewer, with no fragments ending in a comma, and never let a status word stand alone** ("CONFIRMED" opened a line in S2 10-07). (About 25 number findings, plus 5 trailing-comma fragments in S3 10-07.)
- **All text at least ~40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label.** This covers "Lymph nodes", "Flea bite", "Looked viral", "Germ unknown" and status chips. (Long-form tiny text: 141 findings, 17 in L1 10-07.)
- **Keep every box and character inside the safe frame: bottom edge ≤92% of height (y ≤ 994 on 1080, y ≤ 1766 on 1920), with 5% margins at the sides and top. That includes x ≥ 0** ("Report to WHO" started at x=-30 in S1 10-07). Split long pills onto 2 lines. Don't crop mascots at frame edges. (111 long-form off-screen findings; cropped mascots in S3 09-29 and S3 10-07.)
- **Never open on or hold a lone icon on a plain gradient, or a frame showing only chips. Every sampled frame needs a headline plus hero art or a scene covering at least ~25% of the frame.** L1 10-07 had a lone test tube, a lone calendar, a lone "Not confirmed" doctor and an empty "Confirmed/Reported" chip frame. (141 long-form sparse findings.)
- **Keep text off faces, hero art and other elements.** Stat titles and captions go above or beside the hero. Charts get their own box. (About 40 findings, including captions on the lungs art in S2 10-07.)

### Scriptwriters
- **Shorts must run under 90 s, which the system enforces as an automatic drop. Write about 200 words or fewer and aim for about 75 s.** S1 10-07 was held at 91 s. The playbook also wants 35–50 s test cuts.
- **Write narration in normal spelling and every stat as numerals ("Yersinia pestis", "1 in 802", "Phase 2b"). Never use phonetic respellings.** Subtitles are built from the script. (3 holds, plus wrong on-screen figures in S3 10-05.)
- **Tie every claim to the exact form, population and scope the source covers, and match its certainty.** "Can spread person to person" (CDC), not "Yes". "Requires direct and close contact", not "usually". Don't widen a pneumonic-plague statement to all plague (L1 10-07 s39). Don't call inhalation the "usual" route (C1 10-07). Write "WHO European Region", not "Europe". In trials, never move a result from one arm to another. (6 scope holds; 5 certainty findings on 10-07.)
- **Every number gets its comparator, timeframe and absolute figure. Report null and not-proven results exactly.** Write "an average of 7 human cases a year (range 0–17)", not "7 a year". Not confirmed ≠ ruled out. Not proven ≠ "a myth". A change within one group ≠ a difference vs control. (About 33 findings.)
- **State the limits the result depends on, and never make on-screen text stronger or narrower than the narration.** Keep population, design and status on screen. (24 findings; a dropped limitation caused the L1 09-27 hold.)

### Carousel Designer
- **Every stat, range, pictogram or table cell needs a qualifier line saying what it counts**, such as average vs fixed figure, range, timeframe or subgroup. Comparison-table cells keep the source's hedge ("Can, with close contact", not "Yes"; C1 10-07). This also applies to Short stat pills and long-form stat cards. (17 findings.)
- **Every claim slide gets a source tag that actually says that line.** C1 10-07 credited a WHO statement to CDC. Helplines show their real hours, and the title promises only what the slides deliver. (9 findings.)

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube description: plain text only, 1,500 characters or fewer, and the last line complete.** Never paste the packaging JSON or literal "\n" (L1 10-07 hold). The order is: quick answer in 3 lines or fewer, with the status hedge → disclaimer plus "if you're worried, talk to a health professional" → up to 4 sources (short title + full URL) → chapters. Cut chapters first if space runs short. (11 of 12 long-form descriptions broken.)
- **Titles: 100 characters or fewer.** Name the subject, the outcome and the full population, and keep the hedge words: "human", "average", "suspected", "as of [date]", "linked to". S3 10-07 dropped "human" and "average". Attribute agency figures ("CDC: …"). Pay off the title in the content. (1 hold, about 25 findings.)
- **Fill every field (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Threads, pinned comment, thumbnail) and close each one with the disclaimer plus the talk-to-a-health-professional line.** Keep Threads bodies to about 350 characters so the closing lines, the date hedge and the red flag fit under 500. (About 18 findings.)
- **Copy the key facts from the verified summary and use them identically everywhere:** status, dates, counts, scope and "and" vs "or". If a script error carries into the captions, it becomes a hold (S1 09-28, S2 10-01).
- **Carry every hedge, qualifier and red-flag line into every caption, including TikTok, Threads and the pinned comment, and never make a claim stronger.** No "at all", "responds well", "proven" or "real result". Keep "health authorities give preventive antibiotics to close contacts", so it never reads as self-treatment (S2 10-07 Threads). The first line before the fold carries the hedge. (About 75 findings, 9 on 10-07.)
- **Tone: no emojis in plague captions on Meta (S1 10-07), and no hype emojis anywhere (🔥 👀).** No brand or agency hashtags such as #CDC or #WHO, because they suggest an endorsement. No engagement questions that invite people to post myths or links. (7 findings.)

### Everyone
- **Status discipline: write "suspected" or "reported", say "not confirmed isn't ruled out", and date every status ("as of Oct 7, 2026").** S1 10-07 never said the death was a suspected case, S2 10-07 showed "CONFIRMED" on its own, and the L1 10-07 description read "0 plague" without its caveats. Never write "as of September" (3 earlier findings). (13 findings.)
- **Every source tag must say the line, in its own words.** Don't present our conversions, inferences or other agencies' facts as the cited source's. L1 10-07 credited "presumptive" PCR, culture time, "1–3 days", "2 metres" and a 7-day watch to CDC/MMWR, and was held. S3 10-07 credited "rare" to CDC. Don't call a paraphrase "exact wording", and don't put it in quote marks (S2 10-07; Rospotrebnadzor's "pathogenic microorganisms"). (About 45 findings.)
- **Keep the source list clean.** Use direct, stable URLs: link the specific X post, not the profile. No search URLs, and no region-blocked or timed-out links. Use the exact published title, the issue year and a "last reviewed" date for living pages (5 times). (About 40 findings.)
- **Urgency and safety caveats travel with the advice, in every output.** Write "contact a doctor or local health authority urgently", not "see a doctor" (C1 10-07 TikTok; S1 10-07's call to action had no urgency). Tie red flags to possible exposure. Give CDC's full pneumonic warning signs (fever, cough, shortness of breath, chest pain), not just 2 of them (S2 and S3 10-07). Add practical safety caveats, such as "dog flea products can be toxic to cats" (S3 10-07). (About 17 findings.)
- **Say exactly what was measured or reported.** Human cases ≠ animal cases. Average ≠ fixed figure. Modelled ≠ measured. A person under observation ≠ a case. Use the same pronouns throughout for the person who died, and never lead with her name. (About 28 findings.)
