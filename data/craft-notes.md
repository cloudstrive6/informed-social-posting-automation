That is too few to show solid patterns, so the list is short and covers only problems that caused a hold or showed up in 2 or more items. Narration, subtitles and audio masking were clean (WER ≤ 0.026, masking ≤ 0.006). Visual scores were 6.5–6.8, with 1–2 weak shots per Short, but the Visual Critic didn't say what was wrong, so there is no visual rule yet._

### Everyone
- **Say exactly what the study measured.** Don't reword the outcome into something broader. Common swaps to avoid: prevalence → "new cases", activity calories → "your daily calories", a peak-cadence measure → "a 30-minute brisk walk", a modelled or estimated figure → a measured fact. Put "estimated" or "modelled" in the on-screen line, not just in the sources (6 findings across C1, C2 and S1).
- **Use the sample size of the analysis that produced the finding, not the size of the whole cohort.** Example: 64,743 people in the early-death analysis, not the 103,684 who wore trackers. Use that same number in the hook, the body, the captions and the title (caused the C1 hold).
- **Every claim needs a cited source that actually says it.** If a line is "probably true" but no source you cite supports it (e.g. "missed by most home monitors", or cocaine as a stroke risk when the cited CDC page doesn't mention it), either add the source that does say it or cut the line (3 findings, C2 and S3).
- **No absolute claims like "nobody has measured" or "no data exists".** Hedge them, e.g. "few studies have looked at…" (1 finding in S3, but the risk is high in a pipeline with no human check).
- **Source list hygiene.** Copy the paper's exact published title, use a URL that isn't region-blocked (www.cnn.com, not edition.cnn.com) and give the page's current date (3 findings: C1, C2, S2).

### Scriptwriters
- **Keep on-screen text no stronger than the narration.** If the voiceover says "a rough estimate", the caption can't say "a guess". The on-screen text gets fact-checked on its own (S1, part of a held item).

### Carousel Designer
- **Stat and chart slides need a qualifier line under the big number** saying what it counts: self-reported, lifetime, modelled, or share of a subgroup. The big number alone gets misread (3 findings on C2 stat and chart slides, plus 1 on the C1 comparison slide).

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **Before you write, get the key facts from the verified content summary:** sample size, the exact outcome measured and the effect size. Title, thumbnail, hook and every platform caption must all use the same figures. A mismatch on any key fact means REVISE and a hold (2 of 2 holds were packaging REVISE).
- **Don't widen the scope in captions.** If the study covered activity calories, lab conditions or one age group, the caption must say the same (S1 hold, following fact-check scope issues).
