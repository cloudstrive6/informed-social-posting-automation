### Scriptwriters
- **Shorts length: the length test ended on 2026-11-26.**
  - If the task still sets an arm, follow it: tight 90–120 words, standard 135–160.
  - Otherwise write to the topic at 45–70 s.
  - Never go over 160 spoken words.
  - Count every word yourself and log the exact count. It was wrong or unclear in S2 11-23, S2 11-25 and S1 11-27.
  - Scene 1's narration holds the spoken hook (S3 11-22 was held for this).
  - End with "Not personal advice, so ask your prescriber.", then the emergency and don't-stop lines when the topic needs them, then the loop line.
- **Copy the fixed safety lines exactly, and speak and show each one in full.**
  - The emergency line keeps "stop… and get medical help right away" and "healthcare provider".
  - Don't-stop line: "For anything that isn't one of those emergency signs, don't stop or change your medicine on your own." Say "that emergency sign" when only one is named.
  - Pancreatitis wording: "with or without vomiting" for tirzepatide, and "with or without nausea or vomiting" for Foundayo (checked again in L1 11-27 s56).
  - Give the full allergy list.
  - List gallbladder signs in the guide's order: upper stomach pain, fever, yellowing of skin or eyes, clay-colored stools.
  - Fluid tips keep "unless you've been told to limit fluids". This is our own caveat: it gets no source tag and is never described as guide wording (S3 11-27 s6).
  - Never pair a safety line with "before your next dose" or other dose-timing wording.
- **Write narration exactly as it will be spoken, and keep it evergreen.**
  - No digits. Spell out contractions outside the fixed lines.
  - Every spoken number names its comparator.
  - Never write "now", "new", dates, "is requiring", or "the only" for something that can change. L1 11-26 used "now" in s4, s56 and s61; L1 11-27 s62 said "the" where "a" is safer.
- **Keep each finding at its source's exact number, scope, verb and hedge, recaps included.**
  - Non-inferiority against an active drug is weaker than beating placebo. Never call it a "tougher test".
  - "Adverse events" and "adverse reactions" are never "side effects".
  - Postmarketing stays "postmarketing".
  - "May be higher" never becomes "is higher".
  - A median stays "median" on every card.
  - Never add "most", "mostly", "many", "often", "similar", "even" or "a few days".
  - Never present slower stomach emptying as the cause.
  - SURMOUNT-1's comparator is "placebo plus counseling".
  - Never rank doses the trial didn't compare, never call one trial "not a fluke", and never call our own arithmetic "the paper's result".
  - A use that isn't approved (MASH, sleep apnea for Foundayo) says so from the first card.
  - Prediabetes alone is not an approved use.
  - Evidence: about 155 findings.
- **Run the muted-viewer test, starting with the opening card.**
  - The opening card names the drug and shows sentence 1's answer, hedge included. For a head-to-head it also names the comparator and the outcome.
  - Every stat card names the measure (mean or median), the drug and dose, both groups, the full population and the timeframe.
  - Never write "no diabetes" and never drop "weight-related".
  - On-screen text is never narrower or stronger than the narration.
  - Evidence: about 125 findings, most recently L1, S2 and S3 11-27.
