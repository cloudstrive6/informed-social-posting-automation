_Based on 8 QA records from 2026-09-26 and 2026-09-27: 2 carousels, 3 Shorts and 3 long-form videos. 5 of the 8 were held, including all 3 long-form videos. Long-form visuals are the new weak spot: scores were 5.7–5.9, with 24–31 weak shots per video. Across the 3 long-form videos, the layout audit found 66 sparse frames, 28 with off-screen text, 24 with tiny text, 7 with overlaps and 4 with text on the hero art. Shorts scored 6.5–6.8. In Shorts, narration and audio masking were clean. In long-form, narration errors caused 2 holds._

### Motion Designer
- **Keep the headline and the mascot or hero art in separate zones.** Put the headline in the top band. Place the mascot below it and scale it so its face never sits under text. "Headline covers the mascot's eyes" came up in at least 7 L1/L3 notes, and the audit found 4 on-hero findings.
- **Keep charts inside their own box, with the headline and qualifier line outside the chart.** Bars ran through the headline or sub-line in 4 notes across L2 and L3. In one case "One Country, Two Answers" became "e Country, o Answers".
- **Keep decorations, speech bubbles and background icons off text.** This covers question marks, DNA and molecule shapes, room icons and syringes. If they can't be moved, put the text on a solid panel. This happened in about 6 notes across L1, L2 and L3, and the audit found 7 overlaps.
- **Keep every text box inside the safe area, with a margin of at least 8% of frame height at the bottom.** Check the final position after animation, especially for captions and title pills near the bottom edge. The audit found 28 off-screen findings, including a title box that rendered completely below the frame.
- **Don't use tiny text.** Stat captions and labels need to be readable at a glance. Text around 24px was flagged as unreadable. Use at least about 40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label. The audit found 24 tiny-text findings.
- **Every shot needs a headline or label, and hero art that fills at least about 25% of the frame.** Don't use frames with only an icon, or dead transition frames. Frames with 6–12% coverage were flagged every time. The audit found 66 sparse findings, the most common problem.
- **Space out groups of labels and icons so they never touch, including mid-animation.** Labels collided for a moment at 21.3s in L1, and memory-photo icons piled up in L2 (2 findings).

### Scriptwriters
- **Write narration in American spelling and write out tricky numbers as words.** For example, write "recognized", not "recognised", and "twenty", not a numeral the voice can misread. Narration that didn't match the script caused 2 holds (L2 and L3). Long-form WER was 0.032–0.065.
- **Keep on-screen text no stronger than the narration.** The on-screen text gets fact-checked on its own. For example, "a guess" vs "a rough estimate" in S1, and a bare "<5%" when the evidence was only "compatible with <5%" in L3 (2 findings, one in a held item).
- **Include the key limitations named by independent experts or the authors.** For example: "only ultra-processed drinks stayed significant in the subgroup analysis." Then put the advice with the strongest evidence first. Leaving this out was a major finding and caused the L1 hold.
- **Match certainty to the statistics.** If one result is p=0.02 and another is p=0.05, don't present them as equally solid. Label relative risk as relative and give the absolute numbers next to it. Don't compare figures calculated against different denominators (3 findings, L2 and L3).

### Carousel Designer
- **Stat and chart slides need a qualifier line under the big number** saying what it counts: self-reported, lifetime, modelled, or share of a subgroup. The big number alone gets misread (4 findings, C1 and C2).

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube descriptions must be complete.** Write the disclaimer, the "talk to a clinician" line and the sources block first. Keep the chapter list short. Check that the text ends on a complete line. All 3 long-form descriptions were cut off mid-word, with no disclaimer, which counted as a major packaging finding each time.
- **Before you write, get the key facts from the verified content summary:** sample size, the exact outcome measured and the effect size. Title, thumbnail, hook and every platform caption must all use the same figures. A mismatch means REVISE and a hold (C1 and S1 holds).
- **Don't make it stronger or wider than the script.** Keep the script's hedges ("seemed tied", "consistent with", "estimated") and its scope, for example activity calories only or one age group. Name the exact condition, for example "type 2 diabetes", not "diabetes". This came up 5 times across S1, L1, L2 and L3.
- **A thumbnail number needs its referent.** Write "−49% risk", not "−49%" on its own (L1, same pattern as the carousel qualifier rule).
- **Deliver captions for every platform (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook), each with the disclaimer.** The L3 package was YouTube only, so the other platforms couldn't be checked.

### Everyone
- **Say exactly what the study measured.** Common swaps to avoid: prevalence → "new cases", activity calories → "daily calories", peak cadence → "a 30-minute brisk walk", hit rate → "memory", "high" → "highest", a modelled figure → a measured one. Don't paraphrase guideline wording into something stronger ("probabilities, not certainties"). About 12 findings across C1, C2, S1, L1, L2 and L3.
- **Use the size of the analysed sample, not the enrolled or whole cohort.** For example, 64,743, not 103,684; 53, not 54. Use the same number everywhere (C1 hold, plus L2).
- **Every claim needs a cited source that actually says it.** Otherwise, cite the source that does, or cut the line. Examples: "missed by most home monitors", cocaine on the CDC page, ~40% not in the Yoo 2007 abstract (4 findings: C2, S3, L2).
- **No absolute claims like "nobody has measured".** Hedge them: "few studies have looked at…" (S3).
- **Source list hygiene.** Use the exact published title, a URL that isn't region-blocked (www.cnn.com, not edition.cnn.com), and the page's current date or year. When a date matters, keep "carried out" and "published" apart (5 findings: C1, C2, S2, L1 ×2).
