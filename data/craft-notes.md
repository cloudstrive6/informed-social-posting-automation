### Motion Designer
- **Measure masking last, on the final file, and never hand off a mix above 0.02.** This is the #1 cause of holds: 3 holds (0.022–0.061), plus 5 that shipped above 0.02 (0.026–0.037). 5 of those 8 were the 13:00 S2 slot, so check S2 twice. The gate let 0.028 through on S1 11-11, so take your own reading. Duck music under all narration, keep sound effects off spoken numbers and names, and re-measure after any re-render.
- **After the voice render, compare a speech-to-text transcript with the script and regenerate any scene that fails, before you mix.** Check every number, "point", "about", unit and drug name, and look for extra words. Errors so far: "of the day", "tears of petide", "rheumatoid", "one thousand three", "mg" read as "milligrams", a dropped "point" or "about", an extra "the". (2 holds, 9 pieces.)
- **Long-forms: check beat sync on the render, not the plan.** In both holds the plan was clean (69/72 and 74/74 beats on time) but only 83–86% of cuts landed on time after render. Take every cut time from the final narration's word timestamps, never reuse shot lengths from the plan, and fix any cut more than 0.5 s off before you submit. (2 holds.)
- **Build captions from the script's on-screen text field, never from the narration.** Numbers, years and codes must match the stat pill exactly ("126", "6.4", "1,032", "575", "2026", "15–20 g", "GLP-1", "MEN 2", "NAION", "B12"). Before render, search captions for HUNDRED, THOUSAND, POINT, PERCENT, TWENTY TWENTY, spaced or hyphenated letters ("F D A", "M-T-C") and pronunciation spellings ("REH-TAH-TROO-TIDE", "DEG-LOO-DEK", "NAY-ON"). (Wrong or split numbers in about 12 Shorts. Pronunciation spellings caused 4 subtitle-mismatch flags.)
- **Each caption card is one whole clause, 2 lines or fewer, and the only text on screen.** Clear the previous card, tags and bubbles completely (ghost text in 4 Shorts). Never split a word, number, code or trial name across cards or lines ("SEVENTY-/SIX", "GLP-/1", "TYPE / TWO DIABETES"). Never end a card on a comma, dash, colon, THE, A, CAN, MAY, WON'T, NOT, DOESN'T, BEEN, a preposition, POINT, TYPE or a bare number. Keep each stat sentence on one card, and give each list item its own card. (Broken cards in nearly every Short.)
- **Keep every tag inside the frame, off the caption, and at least ~40px.** Shorts: keep tags, chips and bubbles above y 1150, show at most 2 at once, and drop any tag that repeats the caption. Body-part tags (Stomach, Kidneys, Blood sugar, Pancreas) collide most. Never put stat numbers over heads. Long-forms: keep the bottom of every tag at y 1000 or less. Name and bottom labels keep clipping ("Sam, 47", "Single-dose pen", "Orforglipron", "15 mg"), and checklist labels keep rendering at 22–26px. Render every chip the shot list asks for (missing in 7 pieces, latest L2 11-11). (About 300 overlap findings in Shorts. About 200 tiny-text and 180 off-screen findings in long-forms, with 26 and 18 in L2 11-11.)
- **Fill every frame and delete every placeholder.** Each frame needs a headline plus a labelled hero covering at least ~25% of it. Document, label and doctor beats on a plain gradient are usually the empty ones (about 180 near-empty findings in long-forms). The pantry/shelf backdrop, the rounded cabinet strips and the dashed boxes are banned (4 pieces since 11-10, latest L2 11-11). Never put the starfield on a body beat (6 pieces). Match the backdrop to the mood: night → night room, brand → teal gradient, clinic → clinic. Delete ghost bubbles, cropped decorative shapes and debug marks.
- **Draw people as whole characters with a neck and shoulders, and keep each named character the same throughout.** No floating heads, blobs or donuts (about 14 pieces, latest L1 11-11 and S3 11-11). Dana's skin and hair changed between shots in L1 11-11.
- **Make the mascot and its bubble agree with the line.** On risk, "not approved" and caveat lines, use a calm, neutral mascot: no smiles, green checks, "Phew", sweating or crying. Never show a bubble that contradicts the caption (S2 11-11 showed "Not for sleep apnea yet" next to "APPROVED FOR SLEEP APNEA"). (About 8 pieces.)

### Scriptwriters
- **Shorts: stay inside the task's length arm and never go over 160 spoken words.** Tight is 90–120 words, standard is 135–160, counting the safety, Exp F and closing lines. Count the words yourself, because post notes have been wrong (one said 170 when the script had 172). End with "Full breakdown on our channel, link in the description." Until 11-17, 08:00 and 13:00 Shorts put the Exp F subscribe line just before it. Long-forms end with "There's a video I'd watch next, linked in the description." (4 Shorts were over 170 words. 2 were missing their ending lines.)
- **Speak every safety line and also write it in full in the on-screen field.** Order: the don't-switch/stop/change line, then "Not personal advice", then the prescriber line last. An emergency card names its trigger and keeps "right away" (for example, possible pancreatitis → stop and call your healthcare provider right away). Then scope the don't-stop line: "For anything that isn't one of those emergency signs…". For unapproved drugs or uses, write "isn't approved" with no "yet". (6 pieces, including S1 and S3 11-11.)
- **Write narration exactly as it should be spoken, and don't date evergreen pieces.** Write numbers and units as words, and give each decimal its own short sentence. Pronunciation spellings go in the narration only. Never use "now", "this week", "new", "this flu season" or holiday dates (5 pieces, latest L2 11-11 "now"). Help lines cover all four markets, or say "your local urgent care or emergency number".
- **Check every count against the source's own unit (cases, participants or trials).** Each number names its measure, drug and arm, comparator, group sizes and timeframe. Give dose ranges as ranges across doses. (1 hold: S1 10-29. About 38 findings.)
- **Keep each finding at its source's exact scope, verb and urgency, including in recaps.** Observational results are "linked to". Randomized results are "compared with placebo". Keep "may", "potential", "rare", "post-marketing" and "won't settle". Never add "most", "many", "that's why" or "similar". (1 failure: L1 10-30. About 50 findings.)
- **Run the muted-viewer test both ways, starting with the opening card.** On-screen text is never stronger or scarier than the narration, and every reason the narration gives also appears on screen. Every stat card names the drug and dose, both groups, the population and the timeframe. (About 48 findings.)

### Carousel Designer
- **Carousels stay stopped.** If the scheduler asks for one, reuse verified Short text only, use generic drug hashtags (no #Zepbound, #PenDay, #GLP1Journey or #ColdAndFluSeason), don't date the hook, and end on the exact disclaimer plus prescriber line. (All 3 carousels had findings.)

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube descriptions are viewer copy only, finished, and 1,250 characters or fewer before the link.** Line 1 stays empty for the ▶ Watch next link. Then: a quick answer of 3 lines or fewer, the safety block, 1–4 sources, chapters, hashtags. Never put an internal note in the field (L1 11-06 was major). Trim "What you'll learn" and the source labels first. (6 long-forms were over the limit. 3 Shorts filled line 1 or had no sources.)
- **Never hand off a broken or partial description (this contributed to a hold).** Re-read the whole field to its last character. Chapters: one per script section, starting at 00:00, with real timestamps that include the checklist and when-to-get-help sections. If the timings aren't final, leave the chapter block out and say so in the post notes. Never use "MM:SS". (L1 11-11 was cut off mid-word and held. L2 11-10 had placeholder chapters.)
- **Every caption field and description ends on exactly "Not personal advice. Talk to your doctor or prescriber about whether it's right for you." Nothing comes after it.** Hashtags and the don't-stop line go before it. Copy safety and pinned-comment lines word for word from the script, with the emergency scoping, "right away" and "with or without vomiting". Write "medicines", never "meds", and never paraphrase as "Otherwise, …". (L2 11-10 had no disclaimer, major. 4 had hashtags after the disclaimer. Pinned-comment findings in 4 long-forms.)
- **Threads stays at about 300 characters, counting the safety lines.** Write it last, and cut a clause, never a safety line. (Over in about 30 pieces, in nearly every batch.)
- **Copy facts, qualifiers, populations and attributions word for word from the verified script.** That includes "on average", "all doses combined", "small trial", "not yet peer-reviewed", "post-marketing", "the label says", the insulin/sulfonylurea caveat and the full trial population. Our own caveats get their own sentence, never after a source tag. (About 115 findings, in every batch.)
- **Titles, thumbnails and first lines keep the hedge, scope and population, and engagement stays safe.** Keep "possible", "may" and "linked to". The thumbnail never reuses the title's words. Don't call data older than ~3 months "new", and never use "Approved yet?" or "Approved Anywhere?". Engagement lines ask people to save or share only, never an open question (3 pieces). Hashtag only generic names of drugs whose own data the post reports: no brand tags and no trend tags (#guthealth, #MetabolicHealth, #WeightManagement). (About 35 pieces.)

### Everyone
- **Give every result the exact drug scope and trial population it came from.** Class studies say "GLP-1 medicines", and tirzepatide trials stay "tirzepatide". Populations: no diabetes in SURMOUNT-1, -4 and -5, STEP 1, ATTAIN-1 and the retatrutide obesity trials. About 25% of ATTAIN-J had type 2 diabetes. SURPASS-2 is type 2 diabetes, open-label, against semaglutide 1 mg. In the Zepbound pooled data, Study 1 excluded type 2 diabetes and Study 2 enrolled only people with it. The resistance-training RCT enrolled men and postmenopausal women aged 50–70 without diabetes, compared with usual activity, and its results are averages. Key numbers: SURMOUNT-4 randomized 335 vs 335, with +14.0% regain, and placebo still ended 9.9% below starting weight. The pancreatitis review counts 11 participants across 5 trials. Hair loss: 5.4% vs 0.9% (women 7.1% vs men 0.5%; on placebo 1.3% vs 0%). Injection-site reactions: 6–8% by dose vs about 2% on placebo, and serious allergic reactions are post-marketing reports. Oral contraceptives: the label says "may reduce the efficacy" for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase. For semaglutide, name the dose (Wegovy 7.2 mg was approved in March 2026).
- **Before you tag a line to a source, find the sentence in that source that says it. If you can't, remove the tag.** Our own tips, help lines and safety lines get no tag, and only exact printed words go in quotes. The low-blood-sugar figure is in the Zepbound label. The July 2023 review was EMA PRAC's. Name both NANOS and the AAO. The NHS's "more at risk" isn't "more likely". (1 failure: L1 10-30. About 65 findings.)
- **Keep the source list clean.** Fetch every URL first: no 402, 403, 404, login redirects, newsroom pages, search-query pages or DOI redirects. Use the PubMed record for paywalled journals. The Medication Guide and the IFU each get their own working URL, not a PI anchor (5 pieces, latest L2 11-11). Every entry carries its live revised or reviewed date, never just a year. Known dates: Zepbound PI 28 Aug 2026; NHS diarrhoea pages 01 May 2026 and 21 Dec 2023. (About 27 findings.)
