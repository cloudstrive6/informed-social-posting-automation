_Based on 22 QA records from 2026-09-26 to 2026-09-29: 6 carousels, 9 Shorts and 7 long-form videos. 7 were held. Scope errors caused 4 of those holds: C1 and S1 on 09-26, S1 on 09-28, and L1 on 09-29, where a study's drug list left out tirzepatide._

_09-29 was a better day, but it is only one day. L1 scored 6.9 with 12 weak shots (earlier long-form videos scored 5.6–5.9 with 24–49). It had no off-screen text, its description was complete and every caption was there. The Shorts had 0 tag-on-caption overlaps (35 on 09-28), and nothing failed the narration or subtitle checks. Keep following the rules behind those fixes. What still goes wrong: sparse frames, tiny labels, Short stat pills sitting on the hero art (S2 fell to 5.7 and S3 to 5.9), captions split into fragments, and captions or titles that drop the population, comparator or limitation._

### Motion Designer
- **Never open on, or hold, a lone icon on a plain gradient. Keep the backdrop for the whole shot.** Every sampled frame needs a headline plus hero art or a scene covering at least ~25% of the frame. Don't cut from a full room to an empty gradient partway through a shot. There were 118 sparse findings in long-form (still 4 in L1 09-29, including a lone bed and a lone lightbulb), plus bare gradient-and-heart frames in S1 09-29.
- **Keep every box inside the safe frame: the final bottom edge ≤92% of frame height (y ≤ 994 on 1080, y ≤ 1766 on 1920), with a 5% margin on each side. This covers captions and icons, not just pills.** Wrap or shrink long pills. There were 77 off-screen findings in long-form on 09-27/28 and 0 in L1 09-29, so keep doing this. Shorts still slip: the S1 09-29 caption was cut to "A 2 DIABETES. E", and the face icons in S3 09-29 were cropped at the bottom edge.
- **Use text of at least ~40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label.** Chip labels at 23–26px get flagged every time: 53 tiny-text findings in long-form, including 7 in L1 09-29 ("Fat cells", "Blood pressure" chips).
- **Shorts have three separate zones: the headline box, the caption band, and a clear area for stat pills and tags at least 40px from both.** Stat pills and their qualifier lines never go on the hero art. Put them above or beside it. Clear the previous shot's pill before a new one comes in. There were 9 on-hero findings (S1 and S3 09-28, S1 and S2 09-29), and on-hero pills are why S2 09-29 scored 5.7. Tag-on-caption went from 35 findings to 0, so keep that up.
- **Keep text, decorations and speech bubbles off faces, and keep them off each other.** Put the headline in the top band and the mascot below it. Question marks, DNA shapes, pill, syringe and person icons, and bubbles never cross text or eyes. If something can't move, put the text on a solid panel. About 20 notes, most recently a question mark on the mascot's face in S3 09-29.
- **Keep charts in their own box, below the big number, the headline and the qualifier line.** Bars cut through text in 6 notes across 3 long-form videos ("e Country, o Answers", the "4" in "12.8–14.4%").
- **Break Short captions at phrase boundaries, in 2 lines or fewer.** Never leave fragments like "OUT." NOW," or "MEDICINE... CHEATING?". Show numbers the same way in the caption and the pill: "34", not "THIRTY-FOUR". That's 5 notes in S3 09-29, plus S2 09-28 ("nine point one").

### Scriptwriters
- **Write narration in normal spelling, American spelling included: "Wegovy", "Mounjaro", "CagriSema", "semaglutide", "GLP-1", "FDA", "CRP", "recognized".** Never write phonetic respellings. The audio and subtitle checks compare against your literal text. This caused 2 holds and 11 mismatched scenes up to 09-28, with 0 on 09-29. A British spelling still slipped into a stat label in S1 09-29. Write stats as numerals ("34 adults", "9.1%"), and reword any bare number that could be read as a year (L3 hold: "twenty" heard as "two thousand").
- **On-screen text can't be stronger than the narration.** It gets fact-checked on its own, for example "a guess", a bare "<5%" and a flat "not yet published" (3 findings).
- **State the conditions and limits the result depends on: who was studied, for how long, and what the authors say the study can't show.** Examples: "only in people who stayed on it for the full 3 years" (S1 09-29), "only drinks stayed significant" (L1 09-27 hold), "all premenopausal" (S3 09-28), and observational limits (C2 09-29). Put the advice with the strongest evidence first (5 findings).
- **Give every number its comparator and compare like with like.** Say "vs sulfonylurea users" or "6 kg more than after supervised exercise". Label relative risk as relative and give the absolute number for each figure you show, not just one of them. Don't set raw drops next to placebo-adjusted differences, or mix denominators, without a note. Don't present p=0.05 as being as solid as p=0.02 (7 findings: L2, L3, C2 09-28, S1 09-29 ×2, L1 09-29).
- **Give lab values in both mg/dL and mmol/L** (2 findings in L1 09-28).

### Carousel Designer
- **Every stat, range, chart or pictogram needs a qualifier line saying what it counts:** raw vs placebo-adjusted, timeframe, subgroup, modelled, or two separate cohorts (44% / 48%). Pictograms must not round the number up (a 1-in-4 icon for 22.9% was flagged). This also applies to Short stat pills and thumbnail numbers (8 findings: C1, C2 09-26/28/29, S1, L1).
- **Put a source tag on every claim slide, and make sure the tagged source says it.** Attribute paraphrases to whoever actually said them (Healio, not "the authors"), and don't add words the source doesn't use ("capsule"). The title must promise only what the slides deliver (5 findings: C1 09-28 ×3, C1 09-29 ×2).

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube description: 3,500 characters or fewer, in this order:** a quick answer of 3 lines or less → the disclaimer and prescriber line → up to 6 sources (short title + URL) → up to 8 chapters. If it runs long, cut chapters, never sources, and check that the last line is complete. 6 of 6 descriptions were cut off on 09-27/28 (major every time). L1 09-29 was complete, so keep doing it this way.
- **Fill every field for every item:** YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Threads captions, plus thumbnail or cover text. Long-form went out YouTube-only 4 times (09-27/28). Thumbnail text was missing in S1 and S2 09-28 and S3 09-29. C2 09-28 had blank YouTube fields.
- **Every caption, Threads and TikTok included, carries the disclaimer and ends with exactly: "It's a prescription medicine. Talk to your doctor or prescriber about whether it's right for you."** Threads has a 500-character limit, so write the body short enough that both lines fit (7 findings: C2 09-28 ×2, S1 and S2 09-28, C1 and S3 09-29).
- **Before writing, copy the key facts from the verified summary: sample, subgroup vs whole cohort, "and" vs "or", every drug or arm included, the exact outcome and the effect size.** Use them identically everywhere. A scope mismatch means REVISE or a hold (C1 and S1 09-26; S1 09-28, where the whole 313,145 cohort was described as BMI 25–26.9).
- **Carry the script's qualifiers into every caption, and never make a claim stronger.** Keep "on average", the population, the comparator, the timeframe, placebo context, "observational", "seemed tied", "what trials show" and "kept" (not "hide"). Don't add "proven", "well-supported" or "slight/slightly" (~25 findings across 11 items, still 12 on 09-29).
- **Titles, thumbnails and keywords name the population and the comparator.** Examples: "in adults with type 2 diabetes", "veterans with T2D", "vs sulfonylureas", "death from any cause". Use generic names first. Any brand must match the trial population, so don't lead with Ozempic. No weight-loss hashtags, and no emojis that undercut the message, such as 🔥 on a post correcting "fat burning" (11 findings: L1 09-27, S2 09-28, L2 09-28, C1 09-28, S3 09-28, S1/S2/L1 09-29).

### Everyone
- **Name the exact population and the full scope.** SELECT = adults with established cardiovascular disease (prior heart attack, stroke or symptomatic PAD) plus overweight or obesity. Keep "both markers" separate from "either marker". Use the analysed sample size (64,743, not 103,684; 53, not 54). List every drug the study included (Rodriguez 2025 = liraglutide, semaglutide **and tirzepatide**). Scope errors caused 4 holds, including L1 09-29.
- **Say exactly what was measured.** Don't swap prevalence → "new cases", activity calories → "daily calories", hit rate → "memory", appetite → "food noise", or modelled → measured. Say what a "main goal" was (for example, non-inferiority). Don't strengthen guideline wording, and don't put paraphrases in quote marks (~16 findings).
- **Every claim needs a cited source that actually says it.** Otherwise cite the one that does (ATTAIN-2 in The Lancet, FDA's biotin guidance, Newsweek for the 21–73 age range) or cut the line (~12 findings, including S3 and C1 09-29).
- **Pay off the title, keyword and CTA.** If the keyword is about stopping, show what happened to people who stopped. If the title says "5-part checklist", deliver five parts (2 findings: C1 09-28, S1 09-29).
- **Hedge absolute and time-sensitive claims**, for example "no full paper yet as of September 2026". Check event dates against today: EASD 2026 runs 28 Sept–2 Oct (3 findings).
- **Keep the source list clean.** Use the exact published or registry title, a URL that isn't region-blocked (www.cnn.com), the page's current or last-reviewed date, the journal issue year, and a publisher that matches the URL. Keep "carried out" and "published" apart (10 findings, including C2, S2 and L1 09-29).
