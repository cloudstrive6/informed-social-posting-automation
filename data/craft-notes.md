### Motion Designer
- **Shorts: keep the bottom ~35% of the frame free of labels. That space belongs to the caption pill. When the caption changes, fully remove the previous shot's labels, speech bubbles and captions.** Put drug-name, organ and stat tags ("Gut", "Protein", "Type 2 diabetes", "Stomach") above or beside the hero art, never under the caption.
  - Overlap findings: 23 across the 10-05 Shorts and 22 across the 10-06 Shorts.
  - Ghost labels: S2 09-30, S1 10-01, S2 10-05, S1 10-06 ("Organs"), and S2 10-06, which left 6 behind ("Mice: mixed", "Wanting/Liking", "Lab mouse", "Reward neuron", "Sugar reward", plus an old caption).
- **Every number in a caption must be numerals and must match the stat pill exactly. Check each one before export.**
  - Write "119", "1 in 802", "35.4%" and "2026", never "AND NINETEEN", "ONE IN EIGHT" or "TWENTY TWENTY-SIX".
  - Never split a number across two caption screens.
  - Keep captions to 2 lines or fewer, and don't leave fragments ending in a comma.
  - About 25 notes. S3 10-05 put a wrong figure on screen. 10-06 was clean, so keep checking.
- **Text must be at least ~40px on a 1080 frame, or drop the label.** This covers chart pill labels, arm and dose tags, "Fat"/"Water"/"Lean tissue" labels, region badges ("US", "EU regulator") and delta tags. Long-form has had 124 tiny-text findings: 21 (L1 10-05), then 15 (L1 10-06). Shorts badges were also too small in S2 and S3 10-06.
- **Keep every box inside the safe frame: bottom edge ≤92% of frame height (y ≤ 994 on 1080, y ≤ 1766 on 1920), with a 5% margin at the sides and top.** Long pill labels go on two lines rather than running off the right edge. Recent cases: "…& 400 mg" was cut off at the right edge, and "Belgian Blue" sat at y 1035–1100 (L1 10-06). Long-form has had 105 off-screen findings.
- **Never open on, or hold, a lone icon on a plain gradient, and keep one backdrop for the whole shot.** Every sampled frame needs a headline plus hero art or a scene covering at least ~25% of the frame. Recent cases: a lone lock, a lone document and a lone syringe (L1 10-06), and a stat title over falling drops with no hero (S3 10-06). Long-form has had 134 sparse findings. Also remove leftover template chrome (S1 10-06).
- **Keep text off faces, the hero art and other elements.** Stat titles and qualifier lines go above or beside the hero, never on the people illustration. Charts get their own box. Recent cases: "Up to 1 in 10,000" sat on the people (S3 10-06), and the "12 weeks" title sat on the window art (S2 10-06). About 40 notes.

### Scriptwriters
- **Write narration in normal spelling, and write every stat as numerals ("119 hospitals", "1 in 802", "Phase 2b"). Never use phonetic respellings.** Subtitles are built from the script. Respellings caused 3 holds, and spelled-out numbers produced wrong on-screen figures (S3 10-05). Reword any bare number that could be read as a year.
- **Tie every result to the exact arm, dose and part of the trial that produced it.** Before writing, list each arm (e.g. COURAGE lower-dose part 25/75 mg vs higher-dose part 200/400 mg) and the results reported for it. Never move a finding, or a safety rate, from one part to another. If a part reported nothing, say what the release did say qualitatively. This caused the L1 10-06 hold, and S1 10-06 applied lower-dose safety figures to the whole trial.
- **Every number needs its comparator, arm size and estimand, and every relative figure needs its absolute figure.**
  - Give the HR together with the absolute rates.
  - Put placebo next to every rate, including dropouts.
  - Give arm sizes, and say when the release doesn't report them.
  - Give the estimated difference with its CI.
  - Name the way of counting ("everyone randomised" vs "if they stayed on treatment").
  - Replace "many more" with the numbers (28.3% vs 4–10%, L1 10-06).
  - Label relative-to-comparator stats as such (L1 10-06 s37).
  - About 26 findings.
- **Report null, within-group and not-proven results exactly.** A change within one group is not a difference vs placebo. "No significant difference" is not "no drop". Not proven is not "a myth" and not "no effect": if only the mechanism is unproven, say so (S2 10-06). Label primary, secondary and post hoc endpoints. 7 findings.
- **State the limits the result depends on:** who was studied and for how long, confounders the study didn't adjust for, and the limitations experts raise (dropping one caused the L1 09-27 hold). Frame cross-trial comparisons as separate trials. Never attribute our own reading to a source. 12 findings.
- **Every scene carries a source ID that actually says the line. Never invent figures, and on-screen text can never be stronger or narrower than the narration.** Headlines keep the population and design (S3 10-06 left out T2D and "observational" on screen; S1 10-05 left out "overweight or obesity"). 12 findings.

### Carousel Designer
- **Every stat, range, chart, pictogram or bubble needs a qualifier line saying what it counts.** That means raw vs placebo-adjusted, the comparator, the arm and timeframe, the subgroup, or a modelled range. Pictograms must not round up. Within-group changes must be labelled. This also applies to Short stat pills, long-form stat cards (L1 10-06 s40) and thumbnail numbers. 15 findings.
- **Every claim slide gets a source tag that actually says it, plus the funder on industry-funded trials.** Never use a "24/7" layout for a helpline that isn't 24/7. The title promises only what the slides deliver. 8 findings.

### Social Copywriter & packaging
- **YouTube titles: 100 characters or fewer, and they must name the outcome, the arm or dose, the comparator, the timeframe and the full population.**
  - Example: "13.1% weight loss on the top dose" and "4.2% on the 75 mg arm, 52 weeks", never a bare number.
  - Say which number is placebo.
  - For add-on drugs, write the combination: "trevogrumab + semaglutide vs semaglutide + placebo", never "vs placebo" alone. That happened 3 times on 10-06 (title, thumbnail, working title).
  - Attribute regulator figures ("EU regulator: up to 1 in 10,000").
  - Write "overweight or obesity", not just "Obesity".
  - Use "linked to" for observational data, and the generic name first.
  - 1 hold and about 23 findings.
- **YouTube description: 1,500 characters or fewer, counted before you submit.** It was cut off in 10 of 11 long-form videos. L1 10-06 was the first clean one, so repeat what it did.
  - Order: a quick answer of 3 lines or less → disclaimer and prescriber line → up to 4 sources (short title + full URL) → chapters.
  - If space runs short, cut chapters first.
  - No "Key numbers" or "What you'll learn" lists.
  - The last line must be complete.
- **Fill every field and close every caption correctly:** the YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Threads captions, the pinned comment and the thumbnail text. Each ends with the disclaimer plus exactly: "It's a prescription medicine. Talk to your doctor or prescriber about whether it's right for you." Keep Threads bodies to about 350 characters so the closing lines, the date hedge and the red flag fit under 500 (C1 and S1 10-05, S1 10-06). About 16 findings.
- **Before writing, copy the key facts from the verified summary and use them identically everywhere:** sample, subgroup vs whole cohort, the arm or part, "and" vs "or", the outcome and the effect size. A scope mismatch means REVISE or a hold (S1 09-28 and S2 10-01 captions repeated the script's error).
- **Carry every qualifier, hedge, caveat and side-effect line into every caption, including TikTok and the pinned comment, and never make a claim stronger.**
  - Any caption that states a benefit names the common side effects. A missing side-effect line was a major finding in the S1 10-06 TikTok caption, and the L1 10-06 pinned comment had none either.
  - Keep "some people find", "may", "rare", "on average", "Lilly/Regeneron reported", "-funded" and "observational" (C1 10-06 dropped 3 hedges).
  - Keep red-flag lines (S3 10-06 dropped the "other sudden vision changes" line in 2 captions).
  - The first line before the fold carries the hedge.
  - Say "cravings" unless the script frames "food noise" as how people describe it (S2 10-06).
  - No "proven", "expert", "real result", "side-by-side" or "slightly".
  - About 70 findings, 10 on 10-06.
- **Hashtags and tone: no #WeightLoss or #WeightLossJourney, no brand-name hashtags, and no hype emojis (🔥 👀).** 5 findings so far.

### Everyone
- **Name the exact population, the arms and the full scope.**
  - SELECT = adults with established CVD (prior heart attack, stroke or symptomatic PAD) plus overweight or obesity.
  - SURMOUNT-1 = BMI 30+, or 27+ with a weight-related condition, without diabetes.
  - Survodutide's T2D trial = overweight or obesity plus T2D.
  - List every drug studied (Rodriguez 2025 includes tirzepatide).
  - Name the trial dose correctly: Wegovy 2.4 mg, not the Ozempic diabetes dose (S1 10-06).
  - Never apply one group's or one arm's result to everyone.
  - Scope errors caused 6 holds, the latest L1 10-06.
- **Caveats, urgency and side effects travel with the advice, in every output.**
  - Protein advice: "unless your clinician has told you to limit protein (for example, kidney disease)". L1 10-06's pinned comment left this out.
  - Fluid advice: "unless you've been told to limit fluids".
  - Severe tummy pain spreading to the back: 999/911 or A&E/ER.
  - Vision changes: follow the source's wording ("urgent GP/111", "see your doctor promptly"), not "same day".
  - List helplines with their real hours.
  - Every benefit script mentions the common side effects.
  - 12 findings.
- **Say exactly what was measured, and use the paper's figures over the press release's.** Paper 752 vs press release 755. Non-adherence ≠ stopping. Prevalence ≠ new cases, and modelled ≠ measured. Cravings/appetite ≠ "food noise" (C2 09-28, S2 10-06). Attribute "up to" figures. Don't put paraphrases in quote marks. About 27 findings.
- **Every claim needs a cited primary source that actually says it, and the source list must be clean.**
  - Never tag a source on a line it doesn't support: "eat slowly" isn't in Wharton 2022 (S2 10-06), and "same day" isn't in NHS (S3 10-06).
  - Use a direct, stable URL: no PubMed search URLs, region-blocked links or links that time out.
  - Cite the current drug label.
  - Use the exact published title and the issue year.
  - Label "last reviewed" dates on living pages: 4 times (L1 09-30, C2 09-29, L1 10-05, C1 10-06).
  - About 37 findings.
- **Hedge time-sensitive claims against today's date (Oct 2026).** Write "as of Oct 2026", never "as of September": it was still showing in the S3 10-06 caption, and earlier in S1 and S2 10-01. The oral semaglutide (Wegovy) pill has been approved since Dec 2025. Date-hedge pending decisions (CagriSema, Q4 2026) and papers that are "in press". 10 findings.
- **Pay off the title, keyword and CTA.** If the keyword is about stopping, show the people who stopped. If the description promises a benchmark, give the number. 3 findings.
