## Title patterns (YouTube; still our strongest signal)
- **A recognisable subject plus a plain question works best.**
  - Mounjaro 114, tirzepatide fat/muscle 96, survodutide 62, CagriSema 55, semaglutide food noise 32, orforglipron 30 (2 days old or more).
  - Median 58.5 (n=6), against 32 for all Shorts. The long-form version, "Did EloraTZP Beat Tirzepatide?", got 178.
  - Generic titles ("GLP-1 vs older drug" 2, "Is GLP-1 cheating" 6) had a median of 4.
  - Lead with "Plague" or "Pneumonic plague", not "Yersinia pestis" or agency acronyms. Answer within 10–15 seconds.
- **A clear "you" action with named subjects is getting stronger.**
  - The anaesthesia Short ("…Tell Your Anesthesia Team") rose from 57 to 82 (n=1).
  - Plague version: "Possible Plague Exposure? What Health Authorities Do Next". Always tell people to "contact a doctor or local health authority urgently", and never suggest self-treatment.
- **Titles crowded with numbers and abbreviations underperform (n=4).**
  - Survodutide long-form 15, retatrutide long-form 12, trevogrumab 11, ACHIEVE-4 30.
  - Use one number at most, in plain words, with its hedge ("CDC: An Average of 7 Human Cases a Year").
- **Agency or company corrections and dated numbers land near the median.**
  - Lilly/retatrutide 50, NYC needle-sticks 45, EU NAION ("up to 1 in 10,000") 31.
  - Plague versions: "Plague Report in Russia: What WHO Has Said (as of [date])" and "About 200 Contacts Under Observation: How Contact Tracing Works".
- **These underperformed:** feelings or stigma questions (6), teasing a number without stating it (4), vague comparators (2) and obscure subjects (trevogrumab 11). Name both sides of every comparison ("plague vs COVID", "1300s vs 2026").
- **Never use fear-bait** in titles, thumbnails or hooks: no "new Black Death", "pandemic" or doom words. Calm framing with a payoff ("is it curable? yes, if treated early") is what earned our best plague reach.

## Topics to double down on
- Plain search questions with 'plague' first, plus the agency that answers them: 'Can pneumonic plague spread person to person?' and 'Plague symptoms: when to get urgent care (CDC)'. Answer within 10–15 s, then give the urgent-care line. The 'Is Plague Curable? What CDC and WHO Actually Say' Reel reached 102 accounts on day 1, about 14× our Reel median (n=1). Named-subject question titles had a median of 58.5 on YouTube, vs 4 for generic titles.
- A dated status decoder: 'Plague case in Russia: what's confirmed vs suspected (as of [date])'. Credit each line to the body that said it (WHO, the Russian health authorities, CDC). Use a plain question, not a string of numbers. Question decoders have scored 178 (long-form), 62 and 55.
- Comparisons that name both sides: 'Pneumonic vs bubonic plague', 'Plague vs COVID: how each spreads', 'Black Death vs today: what antibiotics changed'
- An action-framed explainer: 'Possible plague exposure? What health authorities do next'. Cover contact tracing, preventive antibiotics given by health authorities, and isolation, using Madagascar 2017 as the example. It follows the format of the anaesthesia 'tell your team' Short, now at 82 and still growing. Never suggest self-treatment.
- Corrections of myths already circulating, with the agency named: 'Is plague airborne like COVID?' and follow-ups to the 'no cure' myth. Never amplify bioweapon, lab-leak or cover-up rumours.
- A dated official number plus how it works: about 200 contacts under observation (as of [date]), and how monitoring and preventive antibiotics stop spread
- Plague today in specific places: 'Plague in the US: an average of 7 human cases a year (CDC, range 0–17)'. Cover pets, fleas and practical prevention, including the warning that dog flea products can be toxic to cats.
- Lab safety explained: how high-containment labs work and what happens after a lab exposure. Use only facts that named sources have reported, credited to them.
