## Long-form (priority order)
1. **Lane C first (signal, n=5, median 47).** When a named next-gen drug gets a new trial result or a regulatory step, publish the long-form the same day with the drug name first in the title. Post its funnel Short at 20:00. We missed this for retatrutide TRANSCEND-T2D-1: the Short reached 253 and no long-form was made.
2. **New hypothesis: under-covered next-gen drugs between news cycles.** EloraTZP, survodutide and orforglipron all did well partly because few channels cover them. On days with no news, test one "What Is [new drug]?" explainer a week on a named pipeline drug that has published human data (amycretin, MariTide, pemvidutide, mazdutide). Generic name first, dated status, no "beat" frame. Score it against the Lane C median.
3. **Lane A (search questions) is middling so far (n=2 plus 1 earlier video).** How Long went from 25 to 26 between day 3 and day 4, so the search build has flattened. It's still above the median. Keep 2–3 a week, title them as tirzepatide how-to or what-to-expect questions (the Strength Training video got 40), and score each one at day 7 and day 28.
4. **Lane B gray-market long-forms: pause after 5 views (anecdote, n=1).** Gray-market works as a Short (median 103) and fails as a long-form, the same pattern SURMOUNT-5 showed (163 as a Short, 8 as a long-form). Make at most 1 more test, and only with a fresh dated FDA, MHRA or Lilly event.
5. **Don't make approved-drug deep dives on a single trial (median 8, n=5).** A topic that did well as a Short doesn't carry over to long-form.
6. **The "Did X beat tirzepatide?" frame isn't what drives views (499, 24, 19).** The drug being new is. Only use "beat" when a randomized head-to-head trial exists.
7. **Titles:** 60 characters or fewer, at most 1 number, no abbreviations. Thumbnails: no signal yet, because layout is confounded with topic.

## YouTube Shorts
1. **Name tirzepatide in the title, generic name first (proven): median 101 (n=13).** Brand-first "Mounjaro" titles got 31 and 32.
2. **Retatrutide gets the highest peaks (n=6:** 253, 196, 120, 70, 38, 15). New results and "what's confirmed?" angles win. Status titles (38) and vague data titles (NSW, 15 at day 1) don't.
3. **Gray-market "what's confirmed?" Shorts (signal): median 103 (n=7).** At most 1 recall Short a week; the second in 2 days got 32. Phrase the title as a question.
4. **Head-to-heads that name both drugs (signal): median 103 (n=5).** Use a different trial each time.
5. **A new Phase 3 result for a named next-gen drug, with the condition in the title:** 253 and 65.
6. **"Honest uncertainty" body-worry Shorts (anecdote, n=1):** macular degeneration got 104 on YouTube and 160 reach on Instagram. Test 2 more (eye, pancreas, gallbladder, hair) before treating it as a pattern.
7. **Muscle works as an outcome question (131, 101), not as a how-to list ("3 Ways…", 28).**
8. **Banned:** "Is X approved yet?" titles (median 38), titles that name no drug (median about 6), observational surgery angles (5 and 2), scare framing.
9. **Underperformers:** orforglipron Shorts (36–39), trevogrumab (31), semaglutide-only titles (median 45.5, n=4).
