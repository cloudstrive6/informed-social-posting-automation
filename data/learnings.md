## Title patterns (YouTube Shorts, n=20: still our strongest lever)
- **A recognizable subject plus one plain number or question works best.**
  - Top Shorts: Smartwatch calories 15–25% (137), Mounjaro metabolism (115), tirzepatide fat or muscle (96), anesthesia (82), plague in the US (69), survodutide (63), CagriSema (56).
  - Generic or abstract subjects did worst: "GLP-1 vs Older Drug" (2), "Is GLP-1 cheating" (6), scented cleaners (4).
- **A "you" action with named subjects works** (anesthesia, 82).
  - Plague version: "Possible plague exposure? What health authorities do next".
  - Always include the urgent-contact line. Never suggest self-treatment.
- **Use one number at most, in plain words, with its hedge.** Crowded number and abbreviation titles scored 16–30 (trevogrumab 16, ACHIEVE-4 30).
- **These underperformed:**
  - feelings or stigma questions;
  - teasing a number without giving it (HRT "number headlines skip", 5);
  - vague comparators;
  - obscure subjects.
  - Name both sides of every comparison.
- **Never use fear-bait** ("new Black Death", "pandemic", doom words). All of our plague results came from calm titles that the video pays off.

## Instagram / Meta
- **Reels are where plague takes off.**
- **Carousels are dead:** reach 0–2 across all 13 posts, including both plague ones.
- **The two platforms often rank posts differently** (curable: 102 reach vs 13 views; anesthesia: 2 vs 82). Never let one platform overturn a finding from the other.
- **Meta plague copy:**
  - The first line is calm and factual, names the agency, and carries the dated hedge before the fold.
  - No emojis, no alarm words, no #CDC, #WHO or #outbreak.
  - Engagement asks are save/share only.

## Principles (untested by our data; keep them)

- Myth corrections target beliefs already circulating ("no cure", "airborne like COVID") and name the agency.
- Not confirmed ≠ ruled out ≠ myth. Date every status line to the day the body spoke.
