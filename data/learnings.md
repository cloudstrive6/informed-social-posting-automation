## Title patterns (YouTube, n=20 Shorts: still our strongest lever)
- **A recognisable subject plus a plain question or number works best.**
  - Mounjaro 114, tirzepatide 96, plague in the US 67, survodutide 62, CagriSema 55.
  - Long-form: "Did EloraTZP Beat Tirzepatide?" is at 202.
  - Generic subjects ("GLP-1 vs Older Drug" 2, "Is GLP-1 cheating" 6) did worst.
- **A "you" action with named subjects** (anaesthesia "Tell Your Anesthesia Team", 82) works.
  - Plague version: "Possible Plague Exposure? What Health Authorities Do Next". Always include "contact a doctor or local health authority urgently", and never suggest self-treatment.
- **Use one number at most, in plain words, with its hedge.**
  - Titles crowded with numbers and abbreviations scored 11–30 (trevogrumab 17, ACHIEVE-4 30). Long-form stat strings scored 12–21.
- **These underperformed:** feelings or stigma questions, teasing a number without stating it, vague comparators and obscure subjects. Name both sides of every comparison ("plague vs COVID").
- **Never use fear-bait** ("new Black Death", "pandemic", doom words). Our best plague results all use calm framing that pays off the title.

## Principles (untested by our data, keep them)
- The title and the first spoken line share one hook, answered within 10–15 s.
- Myth corrections target beliefs already circulating ("no cure", "airborne like COVID") and name the agency.
- Status lines say "suspected" or "reported", are dated to the day the body said it, and credit that body. Not confirmed ≠ ruled out.
